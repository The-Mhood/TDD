## HOW TO READ THIS CATALOGUE

**Confidence ratings**
- **Verified** — corroborated by multiple independent sources or peer-reviewed scholarship.
- **Reasonably supported** — documented in published references, reputable public lists, or standard modern usage; not always cross-checked against primary archives.
- **Tradition (oral)** — part of recorded Igbo oral tradition / regnal chronicles; internal chronology usually unverifiable and should be treated as approximate, **not** as hard history.
- **Disputed / uncertain** — conflicting or thin sourcing; meanings flagged rather than forced.
- **Restricted / sensitive** — usable only with cultural-consultant and Story review; see §11.

**Diacritics.** Igbo orthography uses sub-dots (ị ọ ụ) and tone marks. Where a form is standard and helpful (e.g. ọgbanje, Ọ̀ràézè Ǹrì), it is shown; otherwise the common Anglicised spelling used in Nigerian publishing (Okwu, Amadioha — consistent with existing game canon) is used. Tone-marking every name is optional for the game's typography; spellings below are stable enough for asset naming.

**Regions.** "Origin" gives community/tradition where known. Igbo is not one dialect: meanings shift (e.g. ọdịnala variants, Idemili vs Idemmili). Documented differences are noted, not flattened.

**Gender.** "M / F / Unised / Title" follows common usage; many Igbo names are unisex or gender-shifted in modern usage.

**Etymology key (components recurring below):** *chi/Chukwu* = God · *nna/nna* = father · *nne* = mother · *ada* = daughter/first daughter · *nwa* = child/of · *ego* = wealth/money · *ugwu/ugo* = eagle/prestige · *ụzọ* = road/way · *udo* = peace · *ikenga/ike* = power/strength · *chukwu/chineke* = God Almighty · *mmili* = water · *ala/ani* = earth (deity) · *ji* = yam · *eke, orie, afor, nkwo* = the four market days ([Nri-Igbo calendar; Onukawa 1998](#sources--bibliography)).

## 11. CULTURAL RELEVANCE & SENSITIVITY NOTES

1. **Chukwu is off-limits.** Never name a character (hero, villain, "god-king") Chukwu/Chineke. God-adjacent *given names* (Chukwuemeka…) are ordinary and safe; the supreme being itself is not a person, not a monster, and not a plot boss. Making Chukwu "a villain to defeat" breaks the ontology and will be read as either ignorant or insulting.
2. **Ala/Ani is the most sacred pillar.** Ezeani (priest of Ani) already anchors the game's moral geometry; do not add people named Ala/Ani, and do not play the earth goddess for horror spectacle. Transgression/punishment arcs are legitimate drama; "evil earth mother" is not.
3. **Ekwensu ≠ Satan.** The precolonial figure is a war/chaos/bargain deity — invoked, feared, respected. The Christian-Satan overlay is a missionary gloss, not Igbo consensus. If the game uses Ekwensu, commit to the ambivalent war-master reading; a fire-and-brimstone "devil" betrays both the research and the readership (Igbo players will notice instantly).
4. **Idemili is claimed identity.** Chapter 4's name is a living Nnobi (Idemili North/South) religious-judicial institution with a documented scholarly record (Ifi Amadiume, *Male Daughters, Female Husbands*, 1987; royal python taboo). Use requires credit to the tradition and ideally an Anambra cultural consultant; do not dilute her into a generic "river demon".
5. **Ibini Ukpabi is the slavery-trade oracle.** It simultaneously embodied justice *and* served the Aro trade network's violence. Sanitised "wise ancient judge" and cartoon "evil cult" are both distortions. If included, Story + consultant review is mandatory; consider whether it belongs in Chapter 7 (Gates of the Dead) at all.
6. **Alusi Okija must not be used.** The 2004 Okija shrine ritual killings case is a living national wound. There is no responsible creative use of this name/shrine for entertainment. Excluded.
7. **Ogbanje** — handle as grief/illness/love, never mockery. sickle-cell disease has been proposed by clinicians (Ọnwụbalịlị, *Lancet*, 1983) as the biological substrate; religious stigma against affected children has caused real harm. The game should not score points by making ogbanje "scary monsters"; the devastating reading (ezinma/adanna devotion) is both truer and better drama.
8. **Osu caste.** The *osu* (dedicated-servant/outcast) system persisted into living memory. Using osu as a character class without deep, sympathetic treatment would retraumatise; if Chapter 3 (City of the Living Dead) engages it, it needs a dedicated sensitivity pass with Igbo readers. Do not deploy "Osu" as a race adjective.
9. **Masquerades (mmanwụ) & Ekpe.** Authentic masquerade is restricted cultic theatre; unmasking a performer publicly is a serious breach. Depict spectacle (the game's Kingdom of Masks) with a consultant, never as "exposé". Do not reproduce real cult regalia wholesale.
10. **Historical figures.** Jaja, Ahebi Ugbabe, Omu Okwei are usable as *names*; they are also real people whose records (esp. Ahebi's) are contested and whose descendants exist. Evocation ≠ impersonation; avoid putting real living-community words in their mouths without review. Ọ̀balíke/Ìfikuánim similarly demand care (and are title-office names, not personal names).
11. **Modern political resonance.** Contemporary Igbo politicians' names (e.g. Zik, Azikiwe) and the posthumous political readings of Achebe ("Achebe — Chinua") will be read through politics. Avoid them for narrative characters.
12. **Chi / personal god.** *Chi* (a person's guardian/destiny) is conceptually rich (e.g. "onye amara chi ya na-enyere"…) and is **not** a villain slot; it's aspirational interiority. Good for the protagonist's interior arc (Adanna's chi vs. her mother's choice) rather than an enemy.
13. **Attribution.** If consultants validate specific communities (Nnobi/Idemili, Agukwu-Nri, Ossomari), name those communities in credits — fidelity, not flattery.

## 13. GAPS, UNCERTAINTIES & FURTHER RESEARCH LEADS

1. **Nri regnal chronology** is traditional, not documentary; dates vary by source. Treat all regnal years as texture, not citation.
2. **Diacritic stability** — several entries (Ọkeàfọ̀, Ọkólọkwọ̄) carry high dialect sensitivity; consult Nsukka linguists (cf. Iwundu 1994) before in-game typography.
3. **Adoro, Ọgbuagu, Ọnwụ-as-person** — thin/weak sourcing; excluded or flagged.
4. **Women's names of the pre-1800 deep past** are intrinsically under-documented (records skew to male office-holders); the catalogue compensates with title-forms (Omu, Eze-Nwanyi, Ada-compounds) and literary heroines.
5. **Ifi Amadiume's *Male Daughters, Female Husbands* (1987, Zed Books)** is the single best next text for Chapter 4 and the gender architecture of the whole game; obtain/license it for Story.
6. **Achebe (1988) *The Trouble with Nigeria* and Okri (1991)** — tonal models for postcolonial phrasing; not name sources but craft references.
7. **Nnamdi Azikiwe University (Awka)**, **University of Nigeria, Nsukka** — literature/history departments keep the Ojigbo/Udemmili & Nri traditions; request consultants by in-country contract, not by assumption.
8. **Verify Idemili's python-taboo details** at Nnobi before final Idemili chapter lock (Igbo-Ukwu corpus connection still unresolved archaeologically).
9. Re-audit **existing canon names** (Adanna, Ezeani, Okwu) against Story canon before reusing *their* components in new compounds.
10. This catalogue reflects sources public to this researcher as of 2026-11-18; it is **not** exhaustive and must not be cited in marketing as "complete".
